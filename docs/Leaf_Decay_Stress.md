# Short-half-life 2D stress test

Open [the interactive animation](../output/leaf_decay_stress/demo.html). Three panels compare no decay, five-minute decay, and one-minute decay on the same stream. Play or scrub the timeline to inspect retained cuts, recent observations, and local Gaussian contours.

Run:

```bash
julia --project=. examples/leaf_decay_stress.jl
python3 scripts/leaf_decay_stress_report.py
```

Each of three seeds produces 105,000 points across 120 simulated minutes at 1,000 arrivals/minute while active. There are moving/rotating anisotropic Gaussian clusters, abrupt changes at minutes 40 and 90, and no arrivals from minute 60 to 75. Points are clipped to [-0.98,0.98] inside the tree's [-1,1] domain. All methods receive identical data within a seed.

Settings: rank-1 PPCA in 2D, 64 effective new arrivals required to split, 32 retained examples per leaf, 1,024-leaf limit and depth limit 20. Rank 1 plus isotropic noise can represent any positive-definite 2D covariance, but the online scatter truncation remains approximate. This is a behavior test, not evidence of high-dimensional compression quality.

Every 1,000 arrivals a read-only checkpoint evaluates lazy masses at one common time, routes all historical examples through the current tree, and compares estimated leaf weights with exact exponentially weighted weights. Assertions verify mass, weighted mean and probability normalization at every checkpoint. An independent prefix fit verifies that a 15-minute silence multiplies all subtree masses by the expected factor without new arrivals. The callback does not mutate fitting state.

The animation displays seed 31. Summary numbers average three seeds. At an abrupt change, latest-300-point dots can cover more than one cluster; they are observations rather than samples generated by the model. Shading is raw normalized mass per leaf area, not the within-leaf PPCA density or Pólya-smoothed density. The ellipse is an untruncated one-standard-deviation covariance contour, shown only above 0.1% leaf mass.

Results:

| Half-life | Final leaves | Final MiB | Mean leaf-mass TV | Mean distance to latest-minute empirical mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Disabled | 1,024 | 1.072 | 0.0661 | 0.4107 |
| 5 minutes | 669.7 | 0.710 | 0.0309 | 0.1354 |
| 1 minute | 295.3 | 0.315 | 0.0119 | 0.0262 |

The shorter half-life follows this rapidly changing stream more closely. Tracking distance measures weighted-mean lag and sampling variation, not density reconstruction. Leaf-mass TV uses each model's own partition and decay target. No-decay hits the leaf ceiling; finite-decay runs also use FIFO reservoirs instead of the static reservoir policy. Therefore this is a comparison of the implemented policies, not an isolated causal measurement of the decay factor.

Maximum relative mass error was 1.1e-15 and weighted-mean error 3.93e-15. There is no merging or deletion: lower memory comes from fewer created leaves, not removal of old ones. Stale cuts persist. Timings printed by the runner include repeated exact audits and should not be interpreted as pure ingestion throughput.
